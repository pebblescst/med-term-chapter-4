Specialty: Dermatology · Specialist: Dermatologist · Combining forms: derm/o dermat/o cutane/o
3 layers of the skin
Epidermis epi- + derm/o
Outermost layer; stratified squamous epithelium; no blood vessels
Stratum corneum (corne/o) — outer dead cells Stratum germinativum — basal layer; new cells Keratinocytes → keratin (kerat/o) Melanocytes → melanin (melan/o) — pigment
Dermis / Corium derm/o
Middle layer; vascular connective tissue with nerves and glands
Hair follicles (follicul/o) Sebaceous glands (seb/o) → sebum Sudoriferous glands (hidr/o) → sweat Blood & lymphatic vessels Nerves
Hypodermis / Subcutaneous tissue sub- + cutane/o
Deepest layer; adipose (fat) tissue (adip/o); insulation and cushioning
Hair (trich/o, pil/o)
Follicle (follicul/o) — tube-like structure housing hair root
Shaft — visible part above skin
Papilla (papill/o) — base that receives blood supply
Nails (onych/o, ungu/o)
Nail body — visible plate
Nail root — hidden under skin at base
Nail bed — skin beneath the nail body
Lunula — white crescent at base
Eponychium — cuticle; skin over nail root
Lesion — any area of abnormal tissue. Primary lesions arise from normal skin; secondary lesions arise from changes to primary lesions.
Primary lesions — raised/solid
Nodule
Solid, raised lesion >0.5 cm; deeper than a papule
Cyst
Encapsulated sac containing fluid or semi-solid material
Tumor
Large solid mass; may be benign or malignant
Hematoma
Collection of blood in tissue causing raised swelling
Macule
Flat, discolored spot ≤1 cm (e.g. freckle)
Patch
Flat, discolored area >1 cm
Papule
Small solid raised lesion ≤0.5 cm
Plaque
Raised, flat-topped lesion >1 cm; e.g. psoriasis
Wheal
Transient raised area with irregular border; e.g. hive (urticaria)
Primary lesions — fluid-filled
Vesicle
Small blister ≤0.5 cm containing clear fluid (e.g. chickenpox)
Bulla
Large blister >0.5 cm; pl. bullae (e.g. second-degree burn)
Pustule
Blister filled with pus (e.g. acne, impetigo)
Telangiectasia
Permanently dilated superficial blood vessels; visible "spider veins"
Petechia
Tiny pinpoint hemorrhage ≤2 mm; pl. petechiae
Ecchymosis
Bruise; flat hemorrhagic area >3 mm (non-blanching)
Purpura
Group of petechiae; larger hemorrhagic patches
Secondary lesions
Atrophy
Thinning and wasting of skin
Cicatrix
Scar tissue from healed wound
Keloid
Overgrown, raised scar extending beyond original wound margins
Eschar
Dead tissue/thick crust; often from burn or necrosis
Fissure
Linear crack in the skin extending into dermis
Ulcer
Open sore with tissue loss extending below epidermis
Atopic dermatitis
Chronic inflammatory skin condition; associated with allergies and asthma; itchy, red patches
Contact dermatitis
Skin inflammation from direct contact with irritant or allergen (e.g. poison ivy, latex)
Eczema
General term for inflammatory skin conditions with redness, itching, and scaling; often used interchangeably with atopic dermatitis
Seborrheic dermatitis
Greasy, scaly patches on oily areas (scalp, face); "cradle cap" in infants
Cellulitis
Bacterial infection of skin and subcutaneous tissue; red, warm, swollen area that spreads
Folliculitis
Inflammation/infection of hair follicles (follicul/o + -itis); red bumps around follicles
Furuncle
Boil; deep infection of hair follicle forming a pus-filled nodule
Carbuncle
Cluster of furuncles forming a deeper, interconnected abscess
Impetigo
Highly contagious superficial bacterial infection; honey-colored crusted lesions; common in children
Pilonidal cyst
Cyst containing hair at the base of the spine (sacrococcygeal area)
Pruritus
Intense itching; a symptom, not a diagnosis
Candidiasis
Yeast infection caused by Candida; affects skin, mouth (thrush), or genitals
Dermatomycosis
Fungal infection of the skin (derm/o + myc/o = fungus + -osis)
Tinea capitis
Ringworm of the scalp (capit/o = head); causes hair loss patches
Tinea corporis
Ringworm of the body; circular scaly ring-shaped lesion
Tinea cruris
Jock itch; fungal infection of the groin (crur/o = leg/groin)
Tinea pedis
Athlete's foot; fungal infection of the foot (ped/o = foot)
Pediculosis
Infestation with lice (pedicul/i); head, body, or pubic lice
Scabies
Infestation by Sarcoptes scabiei mite; causes intense itching, burrow tracks
Exanthematous diseases
Viral diseases with widespread skin rash; e.g. measles, chickenpox, rubella
Herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1)
Oral herpes; "cold sores" or "fever blisters" around the mouth
Herpes simplex virus type 2 (HSV-2)
Genital herpes; sexually transmitted; recurrent painful lesions
Herpes zoster
Shingles; reactivation of varicella-zoster virus; painful unilateral vesicular rash along dermatomal distribution
Verruca
Wart; benign skin growth caused by human papillomavirus (HPV); pl. verrucae
Acne vulgaris
Common skin condition with comedones, papules, pustules from blocked sebaceous glands
Comedones
Clogged hair follicles; open (blackheads) or closed (whiteheads); pl. of comedo
Alopecia
Hair loss; can be from disease, stress, genetics, or treatment (e.g. chemotherapy)
Hypertrichosis
Excessive hair growth (hyper- + trich/o); beyond normal distribution for age/sex
Keratinous cyst
Cyst containing keratin; sebaceous or epidermal inclusion cyst
Milia
Tiny white keratin-filled cysts; common in newborns; pl. of milium
Seborrhea
Excessive sebum production (seb/o + -rrhea = flow); oily skin
Psoriasis
Chronic autoimmune scaling papular disease; thick, silvery plaques on skin
Anhidrosis
Absence of sweating (an- + hidr/o); inability to sweat normally
Hyperhidrosis
Excessive sweating (hyper- + hidr/o)
Hidradenitis
Inflammation of sweat glands (hidraden/o + -itis)
Onychia
Inflammation of the nail (onych/o + -ia)
Onychocryptosis
Ingrown nail (onych/o + crypt- + -osis); nail edge grows into surrounding skin
Onycholysis
Separation of nail from nail bed (-lysis = loosening)
Onychomalacia
Softening of the nails (-malacia)
Onychomycosis
Fungal infection of the nail (onych/o + myc/o + -osis); thickened, discolored nails
Paronychia
Infection of the skin beside the nail (par- = beside + onych/o); red, swollen nail fold
Subungual
Pertaining to under the nail (sub- + ungu/o); e.g. subungual hematoma
Pigmentation disorders
Albinism
Absence of melanin pigment; genetic condition affecting skin, hair, and eyes
Vitiligo
Autoimmune loss of melanocytes causing irregular white patches on skin
Dyschromia
Abnormal skin pigmentation (dys- = abnormal + chrom/o = color)
Hyperchromia / Hypochromia
Excessive (hyper-) or decreased (hypo-) skin pigmentation
Cornification & pressure injuries
Callus
Thickened skin from repeated friction or pressure; diffuse area
Corn (clavus)
Localized thickening with central hard core; from pressure on toes
Decubitus ulcer
Pressure sore / bedsore; tissue breakdown from prolonged pressure on bony prominences
Ichthyosis
Abnormal fish-like scaling of the skin (ichthy/o = fish); hereditary
Xeroderma
Abnormally dry skin (xer/o = dry + derm/o)
Benign growths
Nevus
Mole; benign pigmented lesion; pl. nevi. Dysplastic nevi have irregular features
Angioma / Hemangioma
Benign vascular tumor; "strawberry mark"; hemangioma = blood vessel tumor
Dermatofibroma
Benign fibrous nodule in the dermis; firm, slightly raised, brownish
Lipoma
Benign fatty tumor in subcutaneous tissue (lip/o = fat)
Seborrheic keratosis
Benign waxy, "stuck-on" brown growths; common in older adults
Skin tag (acrochordon)
Soft, benign pedunculated skin overgrowth; common on neck and axillae
Malignant neoplasms
Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)
Most common skin cancer; arises from stratum germinativum; slow-growing, rarely metastasizes
Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)
Second most common; arises from keratinocytes; more aggressive than BCC; can metastasize
Malignant melanoma
Most dangerous; arises from melanocytes (melan/o); highly metastatic; use ABCDE rule
Kaposi sarcoma (KS)
Malignant vascular tumor; associated with HIV/AIDS; purple/brown lesions on skin

Burn degrees — depth classification
1st degree
Superficial thickness
Epidermis only; red, dry, painful; e.g. mild sunburn. No blistering.
2nd degree
superficial partial
Superficial partial thickness
Epidermis + upper dermis; red, moist, blistered, very painful.
2nd degree
deep partial
Deep partial thickness
Epidermis + deep dermis; pale/mottled, less painful (nerve damage begins).
3rd degree
Full thickness
Entire skin destroyed; white/brown/black, leathery; painless (nerves destroyed); requires grafting.
4th degree
Deep full thickness
Extends through skin into underlying fat, muscle, and bone.
Rule of Nines — BSA estimation
Head & neck = 9%
Each arm = 9% (×2 = 18%)
Chest (anterior trunk) = 18%
Back (posterior trunk) = 18%
Each leg = 18% (×2 = 36%)
Perineum = 1%
Total = 100%. Used to estimate extent of burns for fluid replacement calculations.
Eschar & escharotomy
Eschar — thick, leathery dead tissue that forms over a full-thickness burn

Escharotomy — surgical incision through eschar to relieve pressure and restore circulation (eschar + -tomy = incision)
Diagnostic procedures
Biopsy types
Excisional biopsy
Entire lesion removed and examined
Incisional biopsy
Portion of lesion removed for examination
Needle aspiration
Fluid withdrawn from lesion via needle
Punch biopsy
Circular tool removes full-thickness skin core
Lab tests
Tzanck test
Smear from vesicle base to detect herpes virus
Wood's light examination
UV light exam; fungal infections fluoresce
TB skin tests
Intradermal (ID) injection; Mantoux/PPD test
Bacterial, fungal, viral cultures
Lab growth of organisms from swab samples
Therapeutic procedures
Tissue removal
Curettage
Scraping of tissue with a curette instrument
Débridement
Removal of dead/damaged tissue to promote healing
Cauterization
Destruction of tissue using heat, electric current, or chemicals
Cryosurgery
Tissue destruction using extreme cold (liquid nitrogen)
Escharotomy
Incision through burn eschar to relieve pressure
Mohs surgery
Layer-by-layer removal of skin cancer with microscopic margin checking
I&D (Incision & drainage)
Surgical opening and draining of abscess
Grafting
Autograft
Patient's own skin used as graft (auto- = self)
Allograft
Donor skin from same species (allo- = other)
Xenograft
Skin from different species; e.g. pig skin (xen/o = foreign)
STSG / Full-thickness graft
Split-thickness includes epidermis + part of dermis; full-thickness includes all layers
Cosmetic procedures
Rhytidectomy
Face-lift; excision of wrinkles (rhytid/o + -ectomy)
Dermatoplasty
Surgical repair of skin (dermat/o + -plasty)
Dermabrasion
Mechanical sanding of skin surface to remove scars or wrinkles
Blepharoplasty
Surgical repair of eyelid (blephar/o = eyelid + -plasty)
Liposuction / Lipectomy
Removal of fat (lip/o); suction-assisted or surgical excision
Pharmacology — routes of administration & drug classes
Routes of administration
Hypodermic (H) — under skin
Intradermal (ID) — within skin layers
Subcutaneous (SC/SQ) — under dermis, above muscle
Topical — applied directly to skin surface
Transdermal (TTS) — absorbed through skin; e.g. patches
Drug classes
Antibacterials Antifungals Antivirals Antihistamines Anti-inflammatories Keratolytics Pediculicides Scabicides Retinoids Emollients Antipsoriatics Immunomodulators Antiseptics Anesthetics
Keratolytic (kerat/o + -lytic) — agent that breaks down or softens keratin; used for warts, calluses, psoriasis.
Pediculicide (pedicul/i + -cide = kill) — kills lice. Scabicide — kills scabies mites.
