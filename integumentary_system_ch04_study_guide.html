Chapter 4 covers the integumentary system — the body’s largest organ by surface area (~2 m²). The skin (cutane/o, dermat/o, derm/o) is the first line of defense, temperature regulator, sensory organ, and vitamin D factory. Specialty: Dermatology (dermatologist).
5 System functions
Defense
First line of defense
Intact skin is the primary barrier against pathogens, UV radiation, chemicals, and mechanical injury. When broken, pathogens gain entry — critical importance in burn patients.
Temperature
Thermoregulation
Sweating (sudoriferous glands → evaporative cooling) and shivering (muscle contractions → heat) plus vasodilation/vasoconstriction in dermal blood vessels.
Sensation
Sensory reception
Dermis contains sensory nerve endings for pain, pressure, temperature, touch, and vibration. Meissner’s corpuscles (light touch), Pacinian corpuscles (pressure), Merkel’s discs (sustained touch).
Vitamin D
Vitamin D synthesis
UV triggers conversion of a skin precursor to vitamin D → essential for calcium absorption (bone health). Melanin blocks UV → darker skin produces less vitamin D per sun exposure.
Elimination
Waste elimination
Sweat (sudoriferous glands) contains water, salts, urea, and uric acid — small amounts of metabolic waste eliminated through perspiration.
A&P terms
TermDefinition
DermisMiddle skin layer (derm/o, dermat/o) = corium. Vascular connective tissue containing blood vessels, lymphatics, nerves, hair follicles, sebaceous and sudoriferous glands. Thicker than epidermis.
EpidermisOutermost layer (epi- + derm/o). Avascular stratified squamous epithelium. Contains keratinocytes (produce keratin) and melanocytes (produce melanin). Renewed every ~28-30 days.
EponychiumThe cuticle (epi- + onych/o + -ium = upon the nail). Fold of skin overlapping the nail root. Seals the space between nail and skin to prevent infection.
Hair follicleTube-like depression (follicul/o) from which hair grows. Extends into the dermis. Contains hair root (living) and papilla (vascular base nourishing growth). Hair shaft = visible dead keratin above skin.
Nail bedEpithelium beneath the nail body (onych/o, ungu/o). Pink color reflects blood supply. Cyanosis of nail beds = hypoxia. Blanching/capillary refill test assesses perfusion.
Nail bodyVisible portion of nail — hard flat plate of dead keratin overlying nail bed. Also called the nail plate.
Nail rootProximal hidden portion of nail embedded in skin fold. The growing portion produced by the nail matrix.
Sebaceous glandsOil-secreting glands (seb/o) associated with hair follicles. Secrete sebum — lubricates skin and hair, inhibits bacterial growth. ABSENT on palms and soles. Blocked → comedone → acne.
Subcutaneous tissueDeepest layer (sub- + cutane/o) = hypodermis. Adipose tissue (adip/o) + loose connective tissue. Insulation, energy storage, cushioning. Site of SQ injections.
Sudoriferous glandsSweat glands (hidr/o, sudor/i, hidraden/o). Eccrine (thermoregulation — all body) and apocrine (body odor — axillae, groin). Anhidrosis = absence; hyperhidrosis = excessive.
Detailed anatomy of the three skin layers, glands, hair, nails, lesion classification, and burn grading.
Skin layers
Epidermis (epi- + derm/o) — outermost, avascular
Stratified squamous epithelium. Key layers: Stratum corneum (corne/o) = outermost dead keratin cells — shed constantly. Stratum germinativum/basale (bas/o) = deepest layer, site of cell division, origin of basal cell carcinoma (BCC). Keratinocytes produce keratin (kerat/o = hard protein). Melanocytes produce melanin (melan/o = dark pigment) — UV protection. No blood vessels — nutrients diffuse from dermis. Renewed every ~28-30 days.
Dermis (derm/o, dermat/o) = corium — middle, vascular
Vascular connective tissue. Contains: blood/lymphatic vessels, sensory nerve endings, hair follicles (follicul/o), sebaceous glands (seb/o — oil), sudoriferous glands (hidr/o — sweat). Papillary dermis (top) = capillary-rich, fingerprint ridges. Reticular dermis (deep) = dense collagen + elastin → skin strength and elasticity.
Hypodermis / Subcutaneous layer (sub- + cutane/o) — deepest
Also: superficial fascia. Adipose tissue (adip/o) + loose connective tissue. Functions: thermal insulation, energy storage, cushioning, anchors skin to underlying structures. Site of SQ/SC injections (insulin, heparin). Not technically part of the skin.
Glands, hair & nails
hidr/o, hidraden/o
Sudoriferous (sweat) glands
Eccrine: most numerous, thermoregulation, watery sweat. Apocrine: axillae/groin, activated at puberty → body odor when bacteria metabolize secretion. Anhidrosis (an- + hidr/o + -osis) = absence of sweating → heat stroke risk. Hyperhidrosis = excessive sweating.
seb/o, sebac/o
Sebaceous (oil) glands
Attached to hair follicles. Secrete sebum → lubricates skin and hair, inhibits bacteria. ABSENT on palms and soles. Blocked → comedone (blackhead/whitehead) → acne vulgaris if bacteria colonize. Seborrhea = excess sebum flow.
trich/o, pil/o, follicul/o
Hair
Hair follicle = tube-like skin depression. Hair root = living portion. Hair papilla = vascular base. Hair shaft = visible dead keratin. Arrector pili = smooth muscle → piloerection (goosebumps). Pilonidal cyst = hair-containing cyst at sacrococcygeal area.
onych/o, ungu/o
Nails
Nail body = visible hard plate (dead keratin). Nail root = proximal hidden growing portion. Nail bed = pink epithelium (reflects blood supply). Lunula = pale crescent (visible matrix). Eponychium = cuticle (epi- + onych/o). Subungual = sub- + ungu/o + -al = below the nail.
Primary skin lesions
Macule
Flat, discolored <1 cm. Freckle, petechiae. No surface change.
Patch
Flat, discolored >1 cm. Vitiligo. Large macule.
Papule
Solid, raised <1 cm. Acne, wart. Small bump with borders.
Plaque
Solid, raised >1 cm. Psoriasis. Large flat-topped papule.
Vesicle
Fluid blister <1 cm. Chickenpox, HSV. Clear fluid, thin-walled.
Bulla
Fluid blister >1 cm. 2nd-degree burn. Large vesicle.
Pustule
Pus-filled lesion. Acne, impetigo. White/yellow content.
Wheal
Transient raised edema. Hives. Resolves within hours.
Petechia
Pinpoint hemorrhage <2 mm. Non-blanching. Thrombocytopenia.
Ecchymosis
Bruise >2 mm. Red→purple→green→yellow. Non-blanching.
Nodule
Solid deeper mass 0.5-2 cm. Lipoma. Deeper than papule.
Tumor
Solid mass >2 cm. Not necessarily malignant — just large.
Secondary skin lesions
Cicatrix
Scar — fibrous tissue after wound healing.
Keloid
Hypertrophic scar beyond wound borders. Rx: steroid injection.
Eschar
Dead leathery tissue over wound/burn. Must be removed (escharotomy or débridement).
Fissure
Linear crack in skin. Dry cracked heels, hand eczema.
Ulcer
Deep skin loss extending through dermis. Decubitus ulcer.
Atrophy
Thinning of skin. Aging, prolonged steroids, chronic sun damage.
Burns — classification
1°
Superficial
Epidermis only. Red, dry, painful. Sunburn. Heals 3-7 days, no scar.
2°
Partial thickness
Epidermis + part of dermis. Blistering (bullae). Very painful. Deep type may need grafting.
3°
Full thickness
All skin layers destroyed. Eschar. PAINLESS (nerve destruction). Requires grafting.
4°
Deep full thickness
Into muscle/tendon/bone. Charred. Amputation may be needed.
Rule of Nines: Head = 9% · Each arm = 9% · Anterior trunk = 18% · Posterior trunk = 18% · Each leg = 18% · Genitalia = 1%. Guides fluid resuscitation (Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA in first 24 hrs).

⚠ Key distinctions
-cide vs -lytic
-cide = substance that KILLS a living organism (pediculicide kills lice; the louse is dead). -lytic = pertaining to DISSOLVING a structure (keratolytic dissolves keratin protein; the protein breaks down). Different mechanisms.
intra- vs trans- (skin routes)
intra- = WITHIN the dermis (ID injection — stays in skin; creates wheal for TB test). trans- = THROUGH all skin layers to circulation (transdermal patch — medication reaches bloodstream through skin).
onych/o vs ungu/o
Both = nail, different origin: onych/o (Greek) → pathological compound terms (onychomycosis, paronychia). ungu/o (Latin) → directional terms (subungual). Know both for exam coverage.
hidr/o vs hydr/o — #1 trap
hidr/o = SWEAT (anhidrosis, hyperhidrosis, hidradenitis). hydr/o = WATER (dehydration, hydrocephalus). One letter difference (i vs y) = completely different meaning. Most frequent spelling error in Chapter 4.

Clinical terms organized by category — bacterial, fungal, viral, parasitic infections, nail disorders, inflammatory conditions, hair disorders, and neoplasms.
Bacterial infections
Folliculitis
follicul/o + -itis
Hair follicle inflammation (Staph aureus). Razor bumps, hot tub folliculitis. Topical/oral antibiotics.
Furuncle (boil)
deep folliculitis
Deep follicle + surrounding tissue → painful pus-filled nodule. Staph aureus. I&D + antibiotics.
Carbuncle
cluster of furuncles
Multiple coalescing furuncles → larger abscess with multiple drainage points. Systemic antibiotics.
Cellulitis
dermal/SC infection
Diffuse dermis + subcutaneous infection (Staph/Strep). Spreading erythema, warmth, swelling. Antibiotics.
Impetigo
superficial, contagious
Honey-colored crusted lesions — children’s faces. Highly contagious. Topical mupirocin or oral antibiotics.
Pilonidal cyst
pil/o + nidal (nest)
Hair-containing cyst at sacrococcygeal area → infected abscess. I&D, surgical excision.
Fungal & parasitic infections
Onychomycosis
onych/o + myc/o + -osis
Fungal nail infection — most common nail disorder. Thickened, yellow/brown, brittle nails. Terbinafine oral (3-6 months).
Tinea pedis
tinea + ped/o (foot)
Athlete’s foot. Itching, scaling, maceration between toes. Also: tinea capitis (scalp), corporis (ringworm), cruris (jock itch).
Candidiasis
Candida yeast
Yeast infection in moist areas (skin folds, oral/vaginal). White patches. Topical azoles; oral fluconazole for severe.
Pediculosis
pedicul/i + -osis
Lice infestation (capitis = head, corporis = body, pubis = groin). Nits = eggs on hair. Rx: pediculicide (permethrin).
Scabies
Sarcoptes scabiei mite
Mite burrows under skin → intense nighttime pruritus. Burrow lines between fingers/wrists. Rx: permethrin cream (scabicide).
Viral infections
Herpes simplex (HSV)
HSV-1 / HSV-2
HSV-1: oral herpes (cold sores). HSV-2: genital herpes. Grouped vesicles. Latent in dorsal root ganglia. Rx: acyclovir, valacyclovir.
Herpes zoster
VZV reactivation
Shingles — reactivation of chickenpox virus → dermatomal painful vesicles. Post-herpetic neuralgia. Rx: antivirals within 72 hrs.
Verruca
HPV wart
Wart from HPV. Types: vulgaris (common), plantar (foot), condyloma (genital). Rx: keratolytics (salicylic acid), cryotherapy.
Inflammatory & immune disorders
Eczema / Atopic dermatitis
dermat/o + -itis
Chronic inflammatory — intense pruritus, dry skin. IgE-mediated (atopic triad: eczema + asthma + allergic rhinitis). Rx: emollients, topical steroids, tacrolimus.
Psoriasis
autoimmune epidermal
Autoimmune → thick silvery-white plaques on elbows, knees, scalp. Not contagious. Rx: topical steroids, biologics, PUVA.
Acne vulgaris
seb/o + follicul/o
Excess sebum + keratin plug (comedone) + C. acnes bacteria + inflammation. Rx: retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, antibiotics, isotretinoin.
Decubitus ulcer
pressure sore/bedsore
Pressure-induced necrosis over bony prominences (sacrum, heel). Stages I-IV. Reposition q2h (prevention). Rx: débridement, wound care.
Seborrheic dermatitis
seb/o + derm/o + -itis
Greasy scales on scalp (dandruff), face. Cradle cap in infants. Rx: ketoconazole shampoo, topical steroids.
Nail disorders
Onychocryptosis
onych/o + crypt- + -osis
Ingrown nail — edge grows into nail fold. Great toe. Improper cutting + tight shoes. Rx: warm soaks, partial nail avulsion for recurrent.
Paronychia
par- + onych/o + -ia
Infection BESIDE nail fold (par- = beside). Acute = Staph aureus. Chronic = Candida. Rx: warm soaks, I&D, antifungal.
Onycholysis
onych/o + -lysis
Separation of nail from nail bed. Causes: psoriasis, trauma, fungal, thyroid disease. White/yellow appearance under nail.
Subungual hematoma
sub- + ungu/o + hemat/o
Blood under nail from crush injury. Dark-blue, painful. Large hematomas need trephination (drainage hole).
Hair, pigmentation & sweat disorders
Alopecia
hair loss
Androgenetic (most common — M/F pattern baldness), alopecia areata (autoimmune patchy), telogen effluvium (stress diffuse), cicatricial (scarring, permanent).
Hypertrichosis
hyper- + trich/o + -osis
Excessive hair anywhere on body. Congenital or acquired (minoxidil, cyclosporine). ≠ hirsutism (androgen-driven female-pattern).
Melanoma
melan/o + -oma
Most deadly skin cancer — malignant melanocytes. ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter >6 mm, Evolution. Wide excision, immunotherapy, targeted therapy.
Nevus
mole / birthmark
Benign melanocyte collection. Plural: nevi. Dysplastic nevus = atypical mole → increased melanoma risk. Monitor with ABCDE annually.
Anhidrosis
an- + hidr/o + -osis
Absence of sweating. Cannot thermoregulate → heat stroke risk. Causes: autonomic dysfunction, skin disease, nerve damage.
Seborrhea
seb/o + -rrhea
Excessive sebum flow → oily skin/scalp. Associated with seborrheic dermatitis (dandruff) and acne.
Ecchymosis
bruise / extravasation
Blood extravasation into skin >2 mm. Red→purple→green→yellow (hemoglobin degradation). Non-blanching. Multiple unexplained → investigate coagulopathy.
Diagnostic and therapeutic procedures, skin grafting, cosmetic procedures, and drug administration routes.
Diagnostic procedures
TermDefinition & notes
TB skin test (Mantoux)Intradermal (ID) injection of 0.1 mL PPD — within the dermis at 15° angle, bevel up → raises a wheal. Read at 48-72 hrs: induration (not redness) ≥10 mm = positive (prior TB exposure).
Punch biopsyCircular 4-6 mm tool removes core through all skin layers. Most common type for histological diagnosis of skin lesions. Sutures close the defect.
Excisional biopsyComplete removal of entire lesion + margin of normal skin — both diagnostic AND therapeutic. Used for suspicious moles, small tumors. Preferred approach for melanoma.
Incisional biopsySample portion of a large lesion for histological exam. Rest of lesion remains — diagnose first, then plan treatment.
Wood’s light examinationUV (black light) exam. Fungi and bacteria fluoresce: tinea capitis (green), erythrasma (coral red). Fast, no samples needed.
Tzanck testSmear from vesicle base → multinucleated giant cells under microscope = confirms HSV or VZV. Fast but largely replaced by PCR.
Tissue removal & wound procedures
TermDefinition & notes
CurettageScraping/scooping tissue removal using a curette (spoon-shaped instrument). For BCC, seborrheic keratoses, warts. Often followed by electrodesiccation (burning the base).
DébridementRemoval of dead/devitalized tissue to promote healing. Methods: surgical (most effective), mechanical, enzymatic (collagenase), autolytic, biological (maggot therapy). Essential for burns and decubitus ulcers.
Escharotomyeschar/o + -tomy = incision into eschar. Emergency for full-thickness circumferential burns — rigid eschar constricts as tissue swells → compartment syndrome. Linear incisions through eschar release the constriction.
Mohs surgeryMicrographic surgery: layers removed + examined under microscope in real-time until clear margins confirmed. Highest cure rate for BCC/SCC. Tissue-sparing — for tumors on face, ears, nose.
CryosurgeryLiquid nitrogen (−196°C) freezes and destroys tissue. For warts, seborrheic keratoses, actinic keratoses, BCC. Ice crystal formation → cell death → lesion falls off in 1-2 weeks.
Incision and drainage (I&D)Surgical incision into abscess to drain pus. Essential for furuncles, carbuncles, infected cysts. Wound left open or lightly packed.
Skin grafting
Autograft
Patient’s own skin (best)
auto- = self. No rejection — best option. STSG (split-thickness): epidermis + partial dermis, can be meshed. FTSG (full-thickness): better cosmetic, smaller areas. Required for large burns.
Allograft
Same species — cadaver (temporary)
allo- = other/same species. Cadaveric skin — temporary biological dressing. Rejected in 10-21 days. Used when patient lacks enough donor skin.
Xenograft
Different species — pig (temporary)
xen/o = foreign/strange. Usually porcine (pig skin). Temporary — rejected in 10-14 days. Reduces evaporative loss and infection while donor sites heal.
Cosmetic procedures
Rhytidectomy
rhytid/o + -ectomy
Facelift — surgical removal/tightening to reduce wrinkles. rhytid/o (wrinkle) + -ectomy (removal).
Dermatoplasty
dermat/o + -plasty
Surgical repair of skin by grafting. dermat/o (skin) + -plasty (repair/reconstruction).
Blepharoplasty
blephar/o + -plasty
Eyelid repair/reconstruction. Removes excess eyelid skin and fat — cosmetic or functional.
Liposuction / Lipectomy
lip/o + -ectomy
Liposuction = suction removal of fat. Lipectomy = surgical fat excision. Both remove adipose tissue.
Routes of drug administration
Intradermal (ID)
intra- = WITHIN the skin
Injection within the dermis. Creates raised wheal/bleb. 15° angle, bevel up, 0.1 mL. TB Mantoux test, allergy testing, local anesthesia. Read TB test at 48-72 hrs — measure induration (not redness).
Subcutaneous (SQ/SC)
sub- = BELOW the skin
Injection into fatty subcutaneous tissue. 45-90° angle, 0.5-1.5 mL. Slower than IV. Insulin, heparin, vaccines. Sites: abdomen, upper arm, thigh.
Transdermal (TTS)
trans- = THROUGH the skin
Medication diffuses through all skin layers into systemic circulation. Bypasses GI first-pass metabolism, sustained release. Patches: nicotine, fentanyl, estrogen, nitroglycerin, scopolamine.
Topical
ON the skin surface
Applied directly to skin surface. Creams, ointments, gels, lotions. Local effect (corticosteroids, antifungals) or systemic (nitroglycerin cream).
Pharmacology highlights
Keratolytics
kerat/o + -lytic = dissolves keratin
Salicylic acid (warts, calluses, psoriasis), urea (xerosis, onychomycosis), lactic acid. Break down hyperkeratotic skin.
Pediculicide
pedicul/i + -cide = kills lice
Permethrin (synthetic pyrethroid), pyrethrin (natural). First-line for pediculosis. Must also remove nits with fine comb.
Retinoids
Vitamin A derivatives
Tretinoin (topical — acne, photoaging); isotretinoin/Accutane (oral — severe acne). Highly teratogenic — requires iPLEDGE enrollment.
Antifungals
Against myc/o infections
Topical azoles (clotrimazole, miconazole) for tinea/candidiasis. Oral terbinafine for onychomycosis. Inhibit ergosterol in fungal cell membranes.
